# Cost and the second incompleteness theorem

Date: 2026-11-20

## Question (verbatim)

> Consider the work of Beklemishev on contraction wrt G2, Willard's results on
> breaking compositionality of proofs, and the lambda-cert formulation that
> explicitly uses a size cost for trust. Can a cost-aware logical framework
> perspective be applied to grasp the essence of G2, and when it does and does
> not apply, in terms of whether or not the ambient logic tracks (or, places
> restrictions on) the cost of using statements in proofs?

## 1. What G2's proof consumes

B–S give G2 an abstract form (Theorems 1 and 3, Remark 3.9). Its proof uses four
things:

1. **A fixed point** `p = ⊠p` ("p says it is refutable"; Jeroslow's variant of
   Gödel's sentence). This is *naming*.
2. **L1**, `□(φ → ψ) ⊢ □φ → □ψ`: *composing* evidence.
3. **L2**, `□φ ⊢ □□φ`: *verifying* evidence, i.e. turning a proof into a proof
   that the proof exists.
4. **□-contraction**, `Γ, □φ, □φ ⊢ ψ ⟹ Γ, □φ ⊢ ψ`: *using one piece of
   evidence twice*.

The fourth is B–S's contribution. Contraction is used in condition C3:
from `⊠x ⊢ □y` and `⊠x ⊢ ⊠y` conclude `⊠x ⊢ ⊠⊤`. The hypothesis `⊠x` is spent
once on each side. Löb's proof has the same step: `□ψ → □(□ψ → φ)` and
`□ψ → □□ψ` are combined, spending `□ψ` twice.

B–S's §4 system S shows that the fourth item cannot simply be assumed. S is
modal K4 over the contraction-free multiplicative fragment of classical logic,
with a fixed point for every modalized formula. In S:
- formalized G2 fails: `□(□⊥ → ⊥) ⇒ □⊥` is unprovable (Prop. 4.1);
- Löb's rule and Henkin's rule are not admissible (Prop. 4.2);
- there are infinitely many inequivalent Gödelian fixed points (Prop. 4.3);
- cut is admissible, and the cut-free proof is *smaller* than the two premises
  together (Theorem 5).

So in S composition is cheap and duplication is unavailable.

## 2. The essence: uniform, free operations on evidence

All three operations (L1, L2, □-contraction) are applied at a **variable**. The
hypothesis `□p` stands for an arbitrary proof of the fixed point, whose size
is not known in advance.

A system that charges for evidence can still compose, verify or copy *a given*
proof, with a budget proportional to that proof's size. What it cannot do is
fix the budget before it knows the proof. The fixed point is exactly the
sentence whose evidence is unknown in advance.

Both of the project's self-justifying constructions show this split.
- **λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀.** For every budget `k`, there is no family `□A ⊸ □□A`, and no
  family `□A ⊸ □A ⊗ □A`, at budget `k` (Prop. 4.4). Each instance exists, at
  budgets `μ_L(□A)` and `2μ_L(A)` (Prop. 4.5). An instance of D2 costs exactly
  `μ(B)`, the price of proving `B` afresh (Theorem 4.6).
- **Willard.** IS(A) keeps modus-ponens closure "in the degenerate case where x
  and y are fixed constants" (TR 93-10, p. 12).

In both, each instance is available and the uniform versions are not.

> **The essence, stated in cost terms.** G2 holds for a theory whose evidence it
> can compose, verify and copy uniformly, with no cost it must account for.
> Self-justification is possible only for a notion of evidence that the theory
> charges for, and cannot uniformly afford to compose, verify or copy.

This is a reading, not a theorem. Its converse is open: that every consistent
self-justifying system with an honest proof predicate charges in this way. The
2026-10-29 note lists it as open; `Willard2005` Remark 4 rests on a sketch.

## 3. Tracking cost versus restricting it

| Ambient logic | Examples | Uniform L1, L2, □-contraction | G2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost-blind | PA, ZF, classical or intuitionistic logic | free | holds |
| Tracks cost, restricts nothing | calf; provability indexed by proof length; step-indexed logic | still derivable; the cost is recorded, not charged against a budget | holds; the cost gives its quantitative form |
| Restricts reuse of formulas | affine or linear object logic (B–S §3; Shamkanov's affine PA; Grišin) | □-contraction returns for provability formulas | holds for arithmetic; fails only in the toy S, and only in formalized form |
| Restricts evidence by growth | Willard: IS_D(A); ISCE(A) | instances only | fails for the charged notion of proof; holds for the free one |
| Restricts evidence by tokens | λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀ | instances only (Props. 4.4–4.6) | fails for certificates (T2: `H°` and `H₁°` are inhabited); holds for codes (P5) |
| Restricts time | the `▷` of TSLC; step-indexing | Löb's rule is a theorem | holds: `▷⊥` is inhabited with 0 rounds left (`sLaterBotZero`) |

### Tracking alone changes nothing that is provable

calf's noninterference (Theorem 2.4) says extensional results cannot depend on
cost. Its cost-erased fragment is an ordinary type theory. So recording cost
cannot change what is provable about provability, and G2 holds unchanged.

What tracking adds is G2's quantitative form:

- **Pudlák 1986.** T proves each finite consistency statement `Con_T(n)` ("no
  contradiction with a proof of length ≤ n") with proofs of size polynomial in
  `n`, and needs size at least `n^ε`. Each finite stretch of self-trust is
  affordable, and its price grows. G2 is the limit: no finite proof pays for
  every `n`.
- **The modal form.** GL is complete for finite, transitive, irreflexive trees.
  Read a world's height as its remaining budget:
  - `□A` holds at a world when `A` holds at every world below it;
  - Löb's axiom is induction on height;
  - `□⊥` holds exactly at height 0;
  - `◇ⁿ⊤` ("consistent n times over") holds exactly at height ≥ n.

  By Solovay's theorem this picture is exact for the propositional logic of
  PA's provability.

The arithmetic meaning of height 0 is a model of `T + ¬Con(T)`. There, T's
proof of `⊥` has nonstandard length: it is a contradiction of unaffordable
cost.

> **G2, in cost terms.** A theory cannot rule out, from inside, a contradiction
> whose length is nonstandard.

### Restricting formulas is the wrong target

B–S's conclusion: "we are still missing convincing examples of mathematical
theories based on weak logics for which G2 would fail". In Shamkanov's affine PA,
□-contraction is admissible (Remark 3.9 and §6), so G2 holds there. Grišin's
set theory goes the same way: extensionality brings contraction back.

My explanation, which is not B–S's, is that `□φ` is `∃p Prf(p, φ)`, and the
proof `p` is a number. A number is a term, and terms may be used any number of
times even where hypotheses may not. Restricting the reuse of formulas leaves
the evidence itself copyable.

### Restricting evidence works, for the charged notion only

**Willard.** The theory does not prove total the growth function that composing
proofs needs. The registry status of each result is given in brackets.
- **Tableaux.** Composition is cut elimination.
- **Hilbert proofs.**
  - No extension of Q verifies its own Hilbert consistency (Pudlák) [cited].
  - The same holds for any theory that proves successor total (Solovay) [cited].
  - Under the *additive* naming convention, ISCE(A) verifies its own Hilbert
    consistency (`Willard2006a` Theorem 3) [`full`].
  - Under the *multiplicative* convention, with Concise Encoding, no consistent
    theory does (Theorem 4) [`full`].

  The naming conventions differ in how large a number a short term can name.

The cost reading explains why each boundary exists. It does not predict where
the boundary falls; that is Willard's work.

**λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀.** The type system restricts evidence, not formulas.
- Codes (`Syn`) are free.
- Each node of a certificate (`R`) consumes one token, and tokens are used at
  most once.
- `reflect` trusts only derivations at a budget below the certificate's size.

The results:
- T1: the calculus is consistent.
- T2: `H°` and `H₁°` are inhabited. This is self-justification in certificate
  form.
- P5: G2 holds for codes.

R4 §4 already says that "□-contraction fails for a quantitative reason — node
count — not a structural one". That answers B–S's open question in the only way
arithmetic leaves open: put the affinity on the evidence, not on the logic.

### Restricting time does not escape G2

`▷` satisfies Löb's rule, since guarded fixed points exist. Its logic is the
budget reading of GL above, so G2 holds. TSLC's time guard locates the budget;
it does not lift G2.

There are two readings of "□ at budget n":
- **(a) Semantic.** `A` holds at every smaller budget. This is GL and `▷`.
  Consistency fails at budget 0, where everything holds vacuously.
- **(b) Evidential.** There is evidence of `A` smaller than `n`. This is the
  2026-10-29 guarded `Tm` and λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀. Trust holds at every budget, because at
  budget 0 nothing is provable, and Löb's rule fails.

PA's provability has (a)'s modal logic (Solovay). Self-justifying systems use
(b). The whole difference is whether provability at budget 0 is vacuously
everything, or nothing.

## 4. Where the cost perspective does not apply

- **Intensional escapes.** Examples are Feferman's consistency for non-Σ₁
  numerations, and Rosser-style predicates. Their L1/L2 fail because the
  predicate does not mean "there is a proof", not because of cost. B–S list this
  as a separate route: weakening the requirements on the proof predicate
  (Feferman, Löb, Jeroslow).
- **G1 and Rosser.** These are recursion-theoretic (B–S Prop. 2.2). No
  derivability conditions are involved, and no cost.
- **Naming.** Every route above leaves the fixed point free. Willard's naming
  conventions are the one place a cost falls on naming. It acts there through
  the size of numbers, not through the diagonal.
- **Locating boundaries.** The lens says there must be a line between
  affordable and unaffordable composition. It does not compute where the line
  falls.

## 5. For the project

- The TSLC verdict stands.
  - The time guard is reading (a), and G2 holds for it.
  - λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀'s size guard is reading (b), and self-justification holds for its
    certificates.
- The cost lens adds one prediction about the project's constructions: those
  whose trust consumes evidence they cannot copy for free can prove their own
  consistency, and the others cannot.

## 6. In calf's own terms (added 2026-11-21, on request)

calf read further: §1 in full (1.1–1.6), plus Theorem 2.4's statement.

- **The calf devices used.**
  - `stepᶜ(M)` incurs cost `c`; costs add.
  - `hasCost(A, e, c) := Σa:A. e = stepᶜ(ret a)`.
  - Cost-aware functions `(a:A) →ᶜ B` have a cost `c(a)` that may depend on the
    input.
  - The extensional phase `¶E` and `◯A := ¶E → A`. Under `¶E`, calf is
    ordinary dependent type theory.
  - Noninterference: a function from an intensional type to an extensional type
    is constant.
  - Values are free to copy.
- **G2 in calf.** Write the checker as a computation `check : Code × Code → F(bool)`.
  - `□A` means some `p` checks, at any cost.
  - `□_c A` means some `p` checks within cost `c`.

  Gödel's three operations become cost signatures:
  - composition costs about the sum of the sizes for Hilbert proofs, and cut
    elimination for tableaux;
  - verification is polynomial in the proof's size;
  - reuse is free.

  Since `c` may be any function of the input, calf accepts the blank cheque. The
  argument goes through, G2 holds, and calf reports the Löb construction's cost
  as a function of the unknown proof's size.
- **The phases.**
  - Under `¶E`, `□_c A` collapses to `□A`, and G2 holds as usual.
  - Pudlák's theorem is an intensional cost signature: `Con_n` has a proof of
    cost polynomial in `n`.
  - G2 says that no fixed cost works for every `n`.
- **Reading, not theorem.** λᶜᵉʳᵗ₀ has a gap of the same shape. `H°`
  (certificates, which bear cost) is derivable. `Con′` (codes, which are free) is
  not (P5), although the two are externally equivalent. Noninterference is the
  calf-side reason such a gap cannot close.
- **Verdict.** calf tracks cost and does not restrict it, so it can price G2 but
  cannot evade it.
