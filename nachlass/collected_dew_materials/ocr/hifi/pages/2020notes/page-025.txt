This notation may look complicated, but the idea behind it is actually quite simple.
Thus Verify( [ ®]) indicates that there is a proof of ® from PA+, while the construct
Double-Verify( [ ® ]) indicates that there is proof of the EXISTENCE of a formalized

proof of statement ® (from again the base axiom system of PA+ ).

Logicians usually don’t worry about the distinction between a verification and a
double-verification because it is usually an unimportant distinction. This is because

PA+ can trivially prove the following fact:

e If ® satisfies Verify( [ ®]) then it also satisfies Double-Verify( [ © ]).

The proof of e follows by an easy induction argument. It is often not explicitly
fomalized in many logic textbooks because the Invariant e is quite obvious. (It
stems from the fact that all proofs, by definition, have finite lengths. This implies that
“double” verifications also do trivially generate finite-sized proofs, whose properties
are also naturally characterized by the Principle of Induction.)
