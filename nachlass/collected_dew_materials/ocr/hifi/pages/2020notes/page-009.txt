We will say an axiom system a can recognize Addition “as a total function” iff
it can prove

Va Vy dz Add(z,y, z)               (18)

Similarly, we will say an axiom system a can recognize Multiplication “as a total

function” iff it can prove
Va Vy dz Mult(z,y, z)                    (19)

Also, we will say an axiom system a can recognize Successor (i.e. the operation of

“plus one”) as a total function iff it can prove

Va 3z Add(z,1,z)                (20)

Some axiom systems a are unable to prove Multiplication is a total function but
can prove every true Aj sentence. Other axiom systems are unable to recognize any
of Addition, Multiplication or Successor as total functions, but they can prove every
valid Aj sentence. It will turn out these facts will be central to understanding the

generality and limitations of Gédel’s Second Incompleteness Theorem.
