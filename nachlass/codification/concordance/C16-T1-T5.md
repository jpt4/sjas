## 0. Method, and what "alone" meant here

A-T requires the five tests to be run "**by someone reading only that
document**". The run was conducted under that constraint literally:

- The only document consulted for **answers** was `codified-sjas.md`.
- `C16-verification-goal.md` was read to obtain the test statements, and
  `ADR-0001-codification-charter.md` §A4 was read **once**, solely to learn
  what T4's two targets are named. Neither was used to supply an answer.
- No registry (`registry/*.md`), no concordance file (`concordance/axes.md`,
  `genealogy.md`, `result-matrix.md`, `replication-map.md`,
  `drift-ledger.md`, `composition-obligations.md`) and no extraction record
  was opened.
- Mechanical greps were run **over `codified-sjas.md` only**, to make
  negative claims ("this string does not occur in the document") checkable
  rather than impressionistic. Each such grep is reported with its result.

This matters because the document is written to *delegate*: it links to the
registries and the concordance at eight points and, in several places, states
outright that the full form of a thing lives elsewhere (§8's "The full
source-anchored grid is `concordance/result-matrix.md`"; §10.4's pointer to
the obligations register). Every such delegation is correct as documentation
design and is, under A-T, exactly the thing being tested. Where a test fails
below, it fails **because** the delegation is load-bearing — not because the
delegated content is wrong.

A second framing note. Two of the five tests have a stricter wording in
ADR-0001 A4 than in the C16 goal's one-line restatement. T2's goal wording is
"proof status and source"; A4 says "proof status and **exact** source". T3's
goal wording is "(profile, apparatus, notion) cell"; A4 says "(profile,
apparatus, **consistency-notion**) cell of the result matrix". The stricter
A4 wording governs below, since A-T is the C16 instrument for discharging A4.

## 1. T1 — State any affirmative system exactly

> **Test.** State any affirmative Willard system exactly: profile + apparatus
> + groups (ADR-0001 A4).

### 1.1 Instance chosen

`IS_D(A)` for `D` = semantic tableaux — the system of `Willard2005`
Theorem 3, chosen because it is the corpus's central affirmative result and
therefore the instance the document is most likely to have been composed
around. A second, deliberately less favourable instance (`ISCE(A)`, on the
Hilbert line) is probed in §1.4 as a control.

### 1.2 The answer producible from `codified-sjas.md` alone

**Language profile — Type-A.** §3.1's four-class lattice assigns Type-A
(successor and addition total, multiplication a 3-way relation `M(x,y,z)`) to
"the whole tableaux line". §6.3's `Normed(a,b)` clause 2 confirms it from the
other direction: the language "employs the eight U-Grounding function symbols
and absolutely no other function symbols", a clause "designed to preclude `α`
from employing a multiplication function symbol — or any of its many
functional equivalents".

**Grounding language — the eight U-Grounding functions.** §3.2 gives the set
for `Willard2005` explicitly: the six Non-Growth functions (subtraction,
division, maximum, logarithm, root, count) plus **addition** and
`Double(x) = x+x`. The Non-Growth condition itself is stated:
`F(a₁,…,a_j) ≤ Max(a₁,…,a_j)`. §3.2 also flags, at the point of use, that
`Root(x,y)` rounds **up** in `Willard2005` (drifts D53, D80) and that
`Willard2006a`'s Non-Growth condition differs (D35) — so the reader is not
misled into thinking the grounding set is uniform.

**Formula classes — the starred hierarchy.** §3.3 assigns `Δ*₀`, `Π*ₙ`,
`Σ*ₙ` (no multiplication symbol; `M(x,y,z)` instead) to `Willard2005`.

**Apparatus — semantic tableaux, eight rules.** §4.4 does the necessary work:
the corpus presents tableaux three ways (drift D54), and the document
**fixes** `Willard2007-APAL` Definition 1's eight primitive rules as
canonical, with a stated reason (`Willard2009` Appendix B invokes them by
number; its Lemma 5 uses rules 6, 7, 8). The two conventions that travel with
the choice are stated rather than left implicit: the root is `¬Φ` rewritten
in **prenex\* form**, and `∀`-elimination terms may mention only
**previously introduced** parameters. §4.1 places tableaux in the apparatus
catalogue as `d_F` (Fitting, Smullyan; tableau elimination rules; **no**
logical axioms), which is what makes §5.4's axiom-versus-theorem sensitivity
intelligible.

**The four axiom groups — §5.1.**

- *Group-0*: axioms fixing `C₀ = 0`, `C₁ = 1`, `C₂ = 2`, plus the scheme
  generating the remaining integers.
- *Group-1*: a **finite** set of `Π₁`-class axioms which, with Group-0,
  proves every **true** `Δ₀`-class sentence and gives `=` and `≤`
  transitivity, reflexivity, symmetry and total-ordering. Explicitly
  characterised by property, not by list: "any finite set with these
  properties will serve; the particular choice is immaterial."
- *Group-2*: the reflection schema `∀y { Prf_A(⌜Φ⌝, y) ⇒ Φ }`, infinite by
  construction.
- *Group-3*: one sentence asserting of Groups 0–2 **together with itself**
  that no `D`-proof of `0=1` exists, built by Kleene fixed point over a
  substitution predicate, and required to admit a **`Π*₁`-class** (not
  `Π*₂`) encoding — §5.1 flags that `Willard2005` stresses this is what makes
  the preservation argument work.

**Affirmative theorem, hypothesis, status, source.** §6.1 gives the
hypothesis in `Willard2005` Definition 1's form: `ℑ(A)` is consistent
whenever **`A`'s `Π*₁` theorems are valid in the standard model**, and warns
(drift D55) that "consistency preserving" is never to be written unqualified
because two other papers attach different hypotheses to the phrase. §6.5
gives the result: **`Willard2005` Theorem 3 (p. 27, `full`)** — `IS_D(·)` is
consistency preserving when `D` is semantic tableaux — immediate from Theorem
1 (p. 19, `full`) and Theorem 2 (p. 26, `full`, `θ = 1/4`).

That is a complete answer at the granularity A4 asks for.

### 1.3 Gaps found

**G-T1-a — Group-0's naming scheme is unspecified off the Hilbert line.**
§5.1 says Group-0 contains "the scheme that generates the rest of the
integers" and adds that "on the Hilbert line it is the *only* thing that
varies between systems (§5.5)". §5.5 then tabulates five naming conventions,
all Hilbert-line. The document never says which scheme `Willard2005`'s
`IS_D(A)` uses. The answer is presumably that the question is idle when
addition is a total function symbol, but the document does not say that
either. Reading the document alone, Group-0 for the central affirmative
system is a named slot with nothing in it.

**G-T1-b — the consistency level of `Willard2005`'s Group-3 is never
stated.** §5.2 makes "what Group-3 asserts" an independent axis with four
values and attributes Level(0−) to `Willard1993-TR` and `Willard2001`,
Level(1) to `Willard2002c`, Level(n) and Level(n+) to `Willard2004`.
`Willard2005` appears nowhere in that table. Its level is inferable — §5.1's
Group-3 wording ("no `D`-proof of `0=1` exists") is verbatim the Level(0−)
row's wording — but inference is not statement, and §5.2's own point is that
the levels are inequivalent for exactly the weak systems under study. A
reader who wants (profile, apparatus, **consistency notion**) for this system
must supply the third coordinate themselves. This is the same defect T3
fails on, seen from the system side.

**G-T1-c — Group-2 is described two ways in three lines.** §5.1's Group-2
heading says "For each `Π₁`-class sentence `Φ` of the base theory, the
axiom …", and the sentence immediately following says the group is "infinite
by construction — **one axiom per theorem of `A`**". Those are different
index sets (all `Π₁`-class sentences of the language vs. the `Π₁`-class
theorems of `A`). The reflection schema is harmless under either reading —
the axiom is vacuous for a `Φ` with no proof — but a reader asked to state
the system *exactly* has to choose, and the document does not.

### 1.4 Control instance: `ISCE(A)`

Run for contrast, because a test passed only on the document's best-composed
system would not be evidence. `ISCE(A)` fares as follows.

*Recoverable:* profile **Type-NS** (§3.1: "the whole Hilbert line");
apparatus **Hilbert-style** in the property sense of §4.1's `Willard2016`
Def. 3.2 ("employs a modus ponens rule and satisfies Gödel's Completeness
Theorem"); Group-0's naming convention **additive**, `C_i = C_{i−1} +
C_{i−1}` (§5.5), together with §5.3's reason it is the right one — the
incremental convention "grows too slowly" to be Continuously Expanding, the
multiplicative "too fast to be self-justifying" (`Willard2006a` pp. 7–8);
Groups 1–3 as in §5.1; grounding = **seven** functions, the six plus
predecessor, under the variant Non-Growth condition `≤ Maximum(2, a₁,…,a_j)`
(§3.2, drift D35); formula classes `Δ⁻₀`, `Π⁻ₙ`, `Σ⁻ₙ` over the grounding
primitives (§3.3); result `Willard2006a` Thm 3, `full`, mechanism "as
Thm 3.4, with additive naming" (§6.9); and the genealogical fact that `ISCE`
**is** `ISREF` with the convention upgraded (§5.5).

*Not recoverable:* the **base-theory hypothesis** under which `ISCE(A)` is
consistency preserving. §6.1's three hypotheses are `Willard2005`'s,
`Willard2016`'s and `Willard2020`'s; §6.9's Hilbert-line table gives a
mechanism column ("minimal-`i` triple `(Ψ, p, i)`") but no hypothesis for
either `ISREF` or `ISCE`, and gives one only for `IS(A)` ("`A` regularly
consistent ⟹ `IS(A)` consistent"), where "regularly consistent" is a term
the document defines nowhere. Also not recoverable: which of `d_E`, `d_M`,
`d_H`, `d_ER` `Willard2006a` uses, and the consistency level (G-T1-b again).

Not pinning the specific Hilbert apparatus is defensible — §4.2's
apparatus-identity thesis is precisely that "all the common apparatuses
produce the same set of final theorems" — but the missing base-theory
hypothesis is not, since it is the antecedent of the theorem.

### 1.5 Verdict

**PASS.** For `IS_D(A)` under semantic tableaux, profile, apparatus and all
four axiom groups are stated, with the language, the formula hierarchy, the
preservation hypothesis, the theorem, its page and its proof status — and
with the corpus's variation at each point flagged rather than smoothed. The
pass is qualified by G-T1-a to G-T1-c and by the `ISCE` control's missing
hypothesis, none of which defeats the test as A4 words it ("profile +
apparatus + groups").

One boundary should be stated so the pass is not read as more than it is.
"Exactly" here means *exact characterisation*, not *literal axiom list*: the
document never writes out Group-1's finite base (deliberately — it is
specified by property), Group-3's fixed-point sentence, or `IQFS`'s "four
**Up-Walking axioms** governing an indeterminate primitive `θ` that walks the
powers of 2" (§5.5), which are named and counted but not given. A reader
wanting to *implement* a system from this document alone could not; a reader
wanting to *identify* one, and know what varies, can.

so the source cannot be located at all. Under the goal's weaker wording
("proof status and source") the verdict would be a qualified pass; under

fail.

## 3. T3 — Read off any (profile, apparatus, consistency-notion) cell

> **Test.** Read off any (profile, apparatus, consistency-notion) cell of the
> result matrix (ADR-0001 A4).

### 3.1 What §8 actually provides

The matrix in §8 is a **two-dimensional** grid: five profile rows (Type-M,
Type-A, Type-S, Type-NS, Type-Almost-M) against five apparatus columns
(Hilbert, Tableaux, TabList, Herbrand, Xtab). There is no consistency-notion
axis. The notion appears, when it appears, as free text inside a cell.

Counting the populated cells — those carrying a `+` or `−` rather than an
em-dash — there are **eleven**. Of those:

- **three** name a consistency notion: Type-A/Tableaux and Type-A/TabList
  ("Level(1)"), and one of the two entries in Type-Almost-M/Herbrand
  ("Level(0^R)");
- **seven** carry a proof status (`full` or `sketch`); the four Solovay-derived
  and naming-convention cells carry none;
- **zero** carry a paper + numbered item + page anchor.

§8's own first line is candid about this: "The full source-anchored grid is
`concordance/result-matrix.md`. Its shape:". The grid in `codified-sjas.md`
is presented as a *shape*, not as the instrument. That is honest design and a
fatal answer to a test that must be run on this document alone.

### 3.2 Instance 1 — (Type-A, Xtab, —): read-off succeeds

Cell reads "**−** `sketch`". Chasing it: §4.5 identifies `Xtab` as
`Willard2020` Appendix's device admitting `℧ ∨ ¬℧` for arbitrary `℧`; §7.3's
"LEM as logical axioms" paragraph gives **`Willard2020` Theorem 4.5 (p. 13,
`sketch`)**: `IS_Xtab(β)` is automatically inconsistent whenever `β` proves
the usual `Π*₁` associativity, commutativity, distributivity and identity
properties. Sign, status, paper, item and page all recovered — but by a
three-section chase (§8 → §4.5 → §7.3), not by reading off a cell. The
consistency notion for this cell is not given.

### 3.3 Instance 2 — (Type-A, Tableaux, Level(1)): read-off fails at the source

This is the test's most natural instance: the corpus's flagship affirmative
cell, and the only one of the eleven where all three coordinates are actually
present in the cell text ("**+** Level(1) `full`").

Answer producible: **affirmative, Level(1), `full`, `Willard2002c`** — where
the paper is not in the cell but must be fetched from §5.2's Level table and
confirmed at §7.5. Numbered item: none (§2.4 above). Page: none. So even the
one cell that carries all three coordinates cannot be traced to the result
that bears the `full`.

### 3.4 Instance 3 — (Type-M, Herbrand, —): read-off produces a contradiction

Cell reads "**−** `full` (**but §7.4**)". Following the pointer, §7.4 says of
the Herbrand column for `Ax-1`/`Ax-2`:

> **threshold** — **not a Willard result**: `Willard2009` p. 18 credits
> Adamowicz–Zbierski [1,3] and Salehi [33]

and adds in prose: "The corpus supplies no Willard proof for the
`Ax-1`/`Ax-2` Herbrand cell." Three problems follow for a reader with only
this document.

1. §8's cell asserts the status `full` for a result §7.4 says the corpus does
   not prove; nothing in the document says who proved it or where, so the
   status has no visible basis.
2. §7.4's table places `Ax-1`, `Ax-2`, `Ax-3` and `Ax-4` in one discussion,
   and §8's matrix files `Ax-3` under **Type-Almost-M**, not Type-M. Whether
   the Type-M/Herbrand cell *is* the Adamowicz–Zbierski/Salehi result, or is
   some other result about genuinely Type-M systems, is not decidable from
   the document.
3. `Ax-1` and `Ax-2` appear nowhere in the matrix at all.

### 3.5 Verdict

**FAIL.** The three-coordinate read-off A4 specifies is not supported: the
matrix has two axes, the third coordinate is present in three of eleven
populated cells, four populated cells carry no proof status, and no cell
carries a source anchor. Two of the three probes returned an answer only by
chasing pointers into §§4–7, and the third returned an unresolvable
inconsistency between §8 and §7.4.

The finding is squarely about `codified-sjas.md` standing alone; §8 declares
the delegation to `concordance/result-matrix.md` in its first sentence, and
that file was not consulted, per §0.

### 4.3 What the document *does* supply, assessed on the merits

The test is a re-derivation test, so the fair question is whether the
material a Willard column and an apparatus audit need is present, even
unlabelled. It largely is, and the apparatus half is the document's best
chapter.

**For an apparatus audit**, §4 is effectively a self-contained one:

- §4.1 catalogues five apparatuses with rules and logical axioms — `d_E`
  (Enderton, modus ponens only, 4-part schema), `d_M` (Mendelson, + generalization),
  `d_H` (Hájek–Pudlák), `d_F` (Fitting/Smullyan, tableau elimination, **no**
  logical axioms), `d_ER` (`Willard2016` Def. 5.6, six schemas, tautologies
  proved rather than assumed "because the problem of identifying tautologies
  is NP-hard") — and defines Hilbert-style **by property** (`Willard2016`
  Def. 3.2: modus ponens plus Gödel Completeness).
- §4.2 states the apparatus-identity thesis in Willard's own voice and draws
  the consequence that matters: the apparatus axis is "not a strength axis…
  It is a **proof-length** axis", extensionally degenerate and intensionally
  decisive, with `Willard2002a` Thm 2.2's "super-exponentially longer" as the
  reason it can carry an incompleteness boundary.
- §4.3 disambiguates "cut" — Gentzen's cut rule vs. the
  Nelson–Pudlák–Wilkie–Paris **Definable Cut**, with the defining three
  conditions — quoting Willard's own two disambiguations.
- §4.4 fixes a canonical tableaux definition with a stated reason (§1.2).
- §4.5 tabulates four devices that recover the cut, the Linear-Sum Effect
  they share, and its three names.
- §4.6 states that apparatus generality is "claimed five times, proved

### 4.4 Why that is not a pass

A re-derivation test asks whether a reader can reproduce a *specific* set of
claims and check the reproduction against the target. With only
`codified-sjas.md`, a reader:

- cannot learn that either target exists;
- has no crosswalk, glossary or correspondence table on which to map Willard's
  vocabulary onto the affine-tree side, so the "column" has no second column
  to be a column of;
- cannot enumerate which claims the 2026-07-28 apparatus audit made, and so
  cannot know whether a §4-derived list is complete, over-complete, or
  divergent — which is the entire content of a re-derivation check;
- cannot detect the one thing the C4 gate exists to detect, namely divergence

### 4.5 Verdict

**FAIL.** The substantive material is present and, for the apparatus half,

### 6.1 The shape of the failures

Four of the five failures share one cause, and it is worth stating separately
from the individual findings: **`codified-sjas.md` is composed as the top
layer of a five-layer system and delegates the index functions downward.**
§8 delegates the source-anchored matrix, §10.2 delegates the registries,
§10.4 delegates the obligations register, and the drift ledger is linked in
"How to read this document" and §10.1 without ever being sized. Every one of
those delegations is declared in the document, correct as documentation
design, and fatal to a test conducted on the document alone.

T1 passes because §§3–6 are *expository* — they state the thing rather than
point at it. T2–T5 fail because they are *retrieval* tests, and retrieval is
the function the document has delegated.

This distinction determines the right disposition. If A-T is read as "the
codified statement must be self-sufficient for retrieval", the four failures
are document defects requiring index work in `codified-sjas.md` — a page
anchor on every table-row result, a source column in §8's matrix, a
consistency-notion coordinate, a concept-to-drift index, and a drift-ledger
row in §10.2. If A-T is read as "the codification *as a whole* must support
these retrievals", the failures are a mis-specified test. The charter's A4
wording — "Sufficiency tests against `codified-sjas.md` **alone**" — and the
goal's A-T gloss — "by someone reading only that document" — are both
unambiguous, so the first reading governs and the failures stand as document
findings.
