## 2. Role in corpus — Bringsjord's "second stage"

Willard records Selmer Bringsjord's advice to split the programme into a
mathematical phase ([34]–[45]) and an epistemological/futuristic phase; this
paper "brings this second stage … to completion." It is the long-form
motivation essay whose compressed published form is `Willard2014`. For the
finite-Group-2 technical result (`IS_D^#(β)` / Kernel / GlobSim), prefer
`Willard2014` §§5–6 (G33). This record captures the motivation layer and the
"Miniaturized Finitism" framing that the WoLLIC chapter shortens.

## 3. Distilled theses

1. **Gödel's caveat `\*` and Hilbert's `\*\*`** remain open to a diluted reading:
   fragmentary self-consistency knowledge is compatible with Second
   Incompleteness's ubiquity (§1).
2. **Two-stage research design** (Bringsjord): keep mathematical novelty
   separate from philosophical interpretation (§1).
3. **Self-justifying** = Part-i + Part-ii; Kleene-style diagonal typically
   kills Part-ii (§2).
4. **Type-M / A / S / NS** by totality axioms; Type-A + tableaux Level-1 and
   Type-NS + Hilbert are the affirmative corridors; Type-S and Type-M close
   them (§2, §7).
5. **Finite compression of Group-2** via kernelized images / GlobSim / L-fold
   strategy (§§5–6; full inventory in `willard2014.md`).
6. **Miniaturized Finitism** (§11): applications-oriented subset of
   mathematics with curtailed growth; addresses the remaining ~10% of
   Hilbert's Second Open Question after Second Incompleteness resolved ~90%.
7. **Type-A vs Type-M growth** (§7): additive doubling `x_{i+1}=x_i+x_i`
   vs multiplicative `y_{i+1}=y_i*y_i` (`y_n=2^{2^n}`); the latter's encoding
   length exceeds atoms-in-universe for `n>100` — engineering suspicion of
   the Utopian diagonalization (§§7–8).
8. **Floating-point / double-precision** multiplication can be total in the
   self-justifying setting while integer multiplication cannot (§7 / Thm 7.1
   here = Thm 4 in `Willard2014`).
9. **Goldstein / Wittgenstein–Platonism compromise**: partial philosophical
   middle ground (§1).
